## Abstract
Mandelbrot demonstrates using cotton price data that financial price changes exhibit far heavier tails than the Gaussian distribution predicts, following instead Lévy stable distributions with infinite variance. Large price moves are far more frequent than Gaussian models imply. Price changes are self-similar across time scales—a property that points toward fractal geometry in financial markets. This foundational work fundamentally challenges the Gaussian return assumption underlying most classical financial models and foreshadows modern extreme value theory and the importance of tail risk management.

## Key Contribution
First rigorous documentation of fat tails in financial returns. Proposed Lévy stable distributions as alternatives to Gaussian. Introduced self-similarity (scale invariance) of financial price processes. Laid groundwork for fractal finance and extreme value theory. Challenged the Gaussian paradigm dominating finance.

## Methods
Analysis of cotton price daily and monthly changes over 60+ years. Q-Q plots and tail analysis. Parameter estimation for Lévy stable distributions (α < 2). Self-similarity analysis across time scales.

## Results
Cotton returns exhibit infinite variance (Lévy stable α ≈ 1.7, not Gaussian α = 2). Extreme price moves occur far more frequently than Gaussian predicts. Return distributions are stable under aggregation but with heavy tails. Volatility clustering visible even at this early stage.

## Critique
Lévy stable distributions (except Gaussian) have infinite variance, complicating portfolio theory. The proposed α index is hard to estimate precisely. Later work showed returns may be better described by finite-variance but fat-tailed distributions (Student-t, power laws). Practical risk management tools developed later (EVT, GARCH).

## Relevance
Every practitioner who uses VaR, stress testing, or extreme event analysis is implicitly responding to Mandelbrot's insight. Fat tails are the central challenge of financial risk management. This paper is the origin of the "Black Swan" intellectual tradition.

## Abstract
Fama synthesizes the empirical literature on market efficiency and formalizes the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH). He defines a market as efficient if prices "fully reflect" all available information and categorizes efficiency into three forms: (1) weak-form (prices reflect all historical price data), (2) semi-strong form (prices reflect all publicly available information, including earnings, dividends, and splits), and (3) strong form (prices reflect all information, including private). Fama reviews the overwhelming evidence in favor of weak and semi-strong efficiency while noting that strong-form efficiency likely fails (insiders earn abnormal returns). This review became the defining paper of the EMH.

## Key Contribution
Formalized the EMH and its three forms. Synthesized empirical evidence on market efficiency. Defined "fair game" and martingale properties formally. Established the EMH as the central organizing framework for financial economics. Defined the terms of debate for 50+ years of subsequent research.

## Methods
Review of random walk tests (filter rules, serial correlation). Analysis of event studies (stock splits, earnings announcements). Examination of mutual fund performance relative to benchmarks. Weak-form tests: variance ratios, autocorrelation. Semi-strong tests: event studies around announcements.

## Results
Weak-form efficiency: strong evidence; filter rules, serial correlation tests fail to find exploitable patterns after transactions costs. Semi-strong efficiency: strong evidence; prices adjust rapidly and completely to public information events. Strong-form efficiency: limited evidence; corporate insiders appear to earn abnormal returns.

## Critique
Tests are joint hypotheses: rejection of EMH could be due to a bad asset pricing model (not market inefficiency). The three-form taxonomy is less clear-cut than it appears. "Fully reflects" is not rigorously defined. Subsequent literature (anomalies, behavioral finance) extensively challenged the semi-strong form.

## Relevance
One of the most cited papers in finance. The EMH is the null hypothesis for all trading strategy research. Every factor paper, anomaly paper, and systematic trading strategy must grapple with Fama's framework. Understanding EMH is prerequisite to understanding alpha, factor premia, and behavioral finance.

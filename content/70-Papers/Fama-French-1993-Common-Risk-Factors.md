## Abstract
Fama and French document that two additional factors—size (SMB: small minus big market cap) and value (HML: high minus low book-to-market ratio)—dramatically improve the ability of CAPM to explain the cross-section of stock returns. Portfolios sorted by size and book-to-market generate factor loadings that capture most of the cross-sectional variation in average returns. The resulting three-factor model explains the majority of variation in portfolio returns and renders CAPM alpha statistically and economically insignificant for most portfolios. This paper became the standard benchmark for performance evaluation and launched the factor investing industry.

## Key Contribution
Founded the three-factor model (MKT + SMB + HML). Provided systematic evidence that size and value are priced risk factors. Replaced CAPM as the standard performance benchmark. Motivated the factor investing / "smart beta" industry. Together with FF 1992, definitively challenged single-factor CAPM.

## Methods
25 portfolios sorted by size (5) × book-to-market (5). Time-series regression of excess portfolio returns on Mkt-Rf, SMB, HML. Factor construction: SMB = average return of small stocks - average return of big stocks (6 portfolios). HML = average return of high B/M - average return of low B/M (6 portfolios). GRS test for joint significance of alphas.

## Results
Three-factor model explains 90%+ of variance in 25 size-B/M portfolio returns. Most portfolio CAPM alphas are eliminated by adding SMB and HML. Factor risk premia: SMB ≈ 0.27%/month, HML ≈ 0.40%/month (historical). GRS test: CAPM strongly rejected; three-factor model not rejected for most portfolios.

## Critique
Data mining / overfitting concerns (factors chosen after observing the data). Value and size premia have declined post-publication. Debate: risk-based (systematic risk) vs. mispricing explanations. Does not include momentum (Carhart 1997 adds it). Value factor has underperformed for extended periods (2007-2020).

## Relevance
The most influential paper in empirical asset pricing. The Fama-French factors (MKT, SMB, HML) are available from Ken French's website and are the universal benchmarks for mutual fund and hedge fund performance evaluation. Every quantitative PM must understand this model deeply.

## Abstract
Carhart extends the Fama-French three-factor model by adding a momentum factor (PR1YR: prior one-year return, skipping the most recent month) to create the Carhart four-factor model. Using a comprehensive database of US mutual funds from 1962-1993, he shows that most apparent persistence in mutual fund performance is explained by common factor exposures—especially momentum—rather than managerial skill. After controlling for the four factors, few funds earn persistent positive alpha. The Carhart four-factor model became the standard benchmark for mutual fund performance evaluation in academia and industry.

## Key Contribution
Created the four-factor model (MKT + SMB + HML + MOM). Showed most mutual fund alpha is explained by momentum factor exposure. Established momentum as a systematic risk factor. The Carhart model is the universal mutual fund performance benchmark. Directly motivated the factor investing industry to include momentum.

## Methods
All CRSP equity mutual funds 1962-1993. Four-factor regression: r - rf = α + β₁(MKT) + β₂(SMB) + β₃(HML) + β₄(PR1YR) + ε. Decile portfolios based on prior year return. Analysis of alpha persistence. Survivorship bias correction.

## Results
Momentum factor (PR1YR) has significant explanatory power beyond three-factor model. Most apparent fund persistence disappears in four-factor model. Top-decile funds: momentum explains most of their outperformance. Persistence declines rapidly: mainly explained by momentum and expenses. Bottom-decile persistence (losers keep losing) more robust.

## Critique
Momentum factor exposure can be difficult to implement (high turnover, transaction costs). Data mining: adding factors always improves in-sample fit. Momentum crashes during market reversals not fully captured. The model treats alpha as static; conditional models may be more appropriate.

## Relevance
The Carhart four-factor model is the standard performance attribution benchmark for long-only equity funds. Every performance evaluation starts by computing Carhart alpha. The momentum factor (UMD/WML) is now included in virtually all factor models. Direct practical application in fund selection and evaluation.

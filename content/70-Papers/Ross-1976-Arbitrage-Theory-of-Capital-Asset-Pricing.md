## Abstract
Ross derives the Arbitrage Pricing Theory (APT) as a less restrictive alternative to CAPM. APT requires only two assumptions: (1) returns are generated by a linear factor model, and (2) no arbitrage opportunities exist in large markets. The no-arbitrage condition implies that expected returns must be (approximately) linear in factor loadings. Unlike CAPM, APT does not require a specific utility function, normal returns, or identification of the market portfolio. The factors are not specified by theory—they can be estimated from the data (statistical APT) or specified a priori (Fama-French, macroeconomic factor models).

## Key Contribution
Founded the APT framework—the basis for all modern multi-factor models. Showed that no-arbitrage alone (without equilibrium) implies return linearity in risk factors. Separated the theoretical content (no-arbitrage → factor structure) from empirical implementation (which factors?). Freed factor modeling from CAPM's restrictive assumptions.

## Methods
Linear factor model: Ri = E[Ri] + bi1·F1 + ... + biK·FK + εi. No-arbitrage argument in large markets: any well-diversified portfolio with zero factor exposures must earn the risk-free rate. Law of one price applied to factor payoffs.

## Results
APT pricing equation: E[Ri] = rf + bi1·λ1 + ... + biK·λK where λk is the risk premium for factor k. Factor risk premia determined by market equilibrium. Multifactor generalization of CAPM's single-factor SML.

## Critique
Does not specify which or how many factors—empirical work must identify them. Approximate (not exact) pricing equation (exact only for large/infinite economies). Factor identification and rotation problems (factors not unique). Testing APT requires strong assumptions.

## Relevance
The theoretical foundation for Fama-French, Carhart, and all commercial factor models (Barra, Axioma, Northfield). Any time someone builds a multi-factor model for expected returns or risk, they are applying APT. Essential for systematic investing, factor investing, and risk model construction.

## Abstract
Jegadeesh and Titman document the momentum anomaly: stocks that performed well over the past 3-12 months continue to outperform over the subsequent 3-12 months, and past losers continue to underperform. A zero-cost strategy of buying the top decile past winners and selling the bottom decile past losers earns approximately 1% per month (12% per year) over 3-12 month holding periods. The effect is robust across subperiods, cannot be explained by CAPM beta or industry effects, and is among the most robust anomalies in empirical finance. This finding directly motivated the WML momentum factor in the Carhart four-factor model.

## Key Contribution
First rigorous documentation of the price momentum effect. Defined the standard momentum formation/holding period methodology. Showed momentum is not explained by CAPM or industry effects. Directly motivated Carhart's momentum factor. Momentum is now the most consistent factor globally after value.

## Methods
NYSE/AMEX stocks 1965-1989. Momentum portfolios: sort stocks by past J-month return, hold for K months (J, K ∈ {3,6,9,12}). Decile portfolios: long top decile (winners), short bottom decile (losers). Returns measured skipping one month between formation and holding. Risk adjustment: CAPM regression, industry decomposition.

## Results
12-3 (12-month formation, 3-month skip, 3-month hold) strategy: ~1%/month. Effect strongest for 6-month/6-month and 12-month/3-month strategies. Not explained by systematic risk (beta). Not an industry effect. Momentum reverses over 3-5 years (long-term reversal; De Bondt-Thaler).

## Critique
Transaction costs: momentum has high turnover, costs can be substantial. Momentum crashes: large drawdowns during market reversals (e.g., 2009). Exposed to short-term reversals if not skipping first month. Behavioral explanations: underreaction to information. Risk-based explanations less convincing.

## Relevance
Momentum is now the most widely implemented factor in systematic equity strategies. The UMD (Up Minus Down) or WML (Winners Minus Losers) factor is part of the Carhart model. Every factor model evaluation includes a momentum factor. This paper is required reading for any systematic quant.

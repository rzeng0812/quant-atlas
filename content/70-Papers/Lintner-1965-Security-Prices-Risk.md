## Abstract
Lintner independently and simultaneously derived the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). Starting from investor optimization over mean and variance, he derives the equilibrium relationship between expected returns and systematic risk. He provides a more rigorous derivation than Sharpe's, showing that in equilibrium the market portfolio is mean-variance efficient and that expected returns are linear in covariance with the market. Lintner also applies the framework to capital budgeting decisions.

## Key Contribution
Independent derivation of CAPM, providing mathematical rigor to Sharpe's intuitive approach. Extended to capital investment decisions. Together with Sharpe (1964) and Mossin (1966), established the CAPM triarchy. Introduced the aggregate supply-demand framework for pricing risky assets.

## Methods
Mean-variance optimization with heterogeneous investors. Market clearing equilibrium. Derivation of the Security Market Line from aggregate portfolio demand. Application to corporate capital budgeting under uncertainty.

## Results
Confirms Sharpe's CAPM: E[Ri] = Rf + βi(E[Rm] - Rf). Provides conditions under which the market portfolio is efficient. Shows that the correct discount rate for capital projects uses beta with respect to the market.

## Critique
Same assumptions as Sharpe's CAPM. Does not address multi-period investment or time-varying risk. Empirical performance is poor (Fama-French 1992).

## Relevance
Provides the foundational derivation of CAPM alongside Sharpe. Important for understanding the theoretical origins of the Security Market Line and the role of beta in asset pricing. Often cited jointly with Sharpe (1964).

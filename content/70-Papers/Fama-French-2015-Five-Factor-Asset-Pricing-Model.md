## Abstract
Fama and French extend the three-factor model by adding profitability (RMW: Robust Minus Weak operating profitability) and investment (CMA: Conservative Minus Aggressive asset growth) factors, motivated by the dividend discount model: stocks with higher profitability and lower investment should have higher expected returns. The five-factor model substantially improves cross-sectional return explanation relative to the three-factor model. However, it fails to explain the momentum premium (Carhart) and the low-volatility anomaly. The five-factor model is the current academic consensus on the equity factor model.

## Key Contribution
Added profitability (RMW) and investment (CMA) factors to Fama-French framework. Theoretical motivation from dividend discount model (not purely empirical). Improved cross-sectional return explanation vs. three-factor model. Established five-factor model as the current academic standard. Factor data publicly available on Ken French's website.

## Methods
25 size × B/M portfolios + additional test portfolios. RMW: long stocks with high operating profitability, short low profitability (sorted on OP = operating income / book equity). CMA: long stocks with low asset growth (conservative investment), short high growth stocks. GRS test for alpha significance. Factor data from 1963-2013.

## Results
Five-factor model: significant improvement over three-factor model. RMW and CMA loadings capture much of the variation unexplained by three-factor model. Small-cap growth stocks (low B/M + high investment + low profitability) have negative alpha even in five-factor model. Momentum not added (acknowledged limitation).

## Critique
Does not include momentum (the Carhart factor)—a significant omission. Fama-French factors are constructed from US data and require updating for international markets. Factor crowding: as more capital flows into these factors, expected returns may decline. Investment factor (CMA) is related to value (HML) and may not add independent information. The value premium (HML) is partially redundant with RMW + CMA.

## Relevance
The current academic standard factor model (five-factor + momentum for many practitioners). Ken French's data library provides daily/monthly factor returns for all five factors. Commercial factor models (Barra, Northfield) incorporate profitability and investment. Required reading for any systematic equity researcher.

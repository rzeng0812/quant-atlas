## Purpose

This is a practical, hand-checked companion to [[Path-Reading-Curriculum|Path Reading Curriculum]]. It mirrors the order of the 30-paper Core Sequence (and, as a bonus, the Phase 3 specialization track papers) so you can track literal progress paper-by-paper, plus it records where a free, legal full-text copy of each paper was found at the time this tracker was built (2026-08-28).

**How to use this:**

1. Check off the `- [ ]` box below when you finish a paper (any pass level counts — even a Pass-1 Reconnaissance skim is progress worth recording here).
2. Separately, open the paper's own note in `content/70-Papers/` and update its frontmatter `status:` field from `unread` to `read`, `working-knowledge`, or `implementation` — matching the three levels defined in the curriculum's "Understanding vs. Working Knowledge" table. **This tracker does not do that for you.** The 226 individual paper notes were deliberately left untouched when this tracker was built; updating `status:` on each paper as you actually read it is a manual, one-file-at-a-time habit, not a bulk edit.
3. The links below point to a free, legitimate full-text copy where one could be found (author/publisher personal pages, university course pages, SSRN, NBER working papers, or similar). Where no such copy exists, the line says so — those papers require JSTOR/library/institutional access. No paywall-bypass or piracy sources are linked here, ever.
4. Links were verified live (HTTP 200) on 2026-08-28 where possible. A handful of university servers block automated requests (bot protection) but work fine in a normal browser — those are noted individually below.

**Coverage:** 27 of the 30 Core Sequence papers have a free link below; 3 (Samuelson 1965, Markowitz 1952, Grinold 1989) had no legitimate free full text findable after a reasonable search and are marked accordingly. Of the additional Phase 3 track papers not already in the Core 30, 7 of 11 have a free link.

## A Note on What "Free and Legitimate" Meant Here

Every link above is either the paper's own author hosting a copy on their personal/institutional page, a university course page mirroring assigned reading, an SSRN or NBER working-paper version, or a public-record filing that happens to include the paper as an exhibit. None of these are paywall-circumvention services — no Sci-Hub, no Library Genesis, no similar site was used or considered. Where none of those legitimate categories turned up a copy, the paper is marked "none found" rather than guessed at. Institutional/library/JSTOR access is the honest answer for those.

Links were live (HTTP 200) when checked on 2026-08-28. Personal and university pages occasionally move or disappear — if a link breaks, searching `"<exact paper title>" pdf` plus the author's name and institution usually turns up either the same mirror relocated or a new one.
